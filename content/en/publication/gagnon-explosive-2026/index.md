abstract: Approximately 50% of children with Tourette syndrome (TS) experience explosive outbursts marked by disproportionate aggressive episodes. Although among the most  disruptive manifestations of TS, their impact on family functioning remains  unclear. This study examined whether explosive outbursts predicted impairments  across dimensions of family functioning. A total of 289 parents of children aged  6-14 (M = 10.6; 75% boys) with TS completed measures assessing tic severity,  explosive outbursts, and family functioning. Hierarchical regressions controlling  for tics and comorbidities showed that explosive outburst severity significantly  predicted impairments in problem solving, role distribution, behavior control,  and general functioning, but not in affective responsiveness, affective  involvement, or communication. Tics and comorbidities were not significant  predictors of any family functioning dimension. Findings suggest that explosive  outbursts contribute to organizational and regulatory difficulties, underscoring  the importance of targeting these processes in interventions. The absence of  associations between explosive outbursts and affective dimensions highlights  their distinct clinical profile.
